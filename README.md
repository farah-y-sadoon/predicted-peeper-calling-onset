# Peeping beyond the pond: transferability of a thermal threshold for spring peeper calling onset, and the limits of large-scale call surveys

Lovett (2013) identified a thermal accumulation index (44.3 degree-days above 3°C from February 1, TS3) as the best predictor of spring peeper (_Pseudacris crucifer_) calling onset at a single pond in New York, but whether this threshold transfers beyond that site is unknown. I implemented this model as a per-pixel raster computation in Google Earth Engine using ERA5-Land reanalysis climate data, extracted predicted onset dates at North American Amphibian Monitoring Program (NAAMP) survey routes, and compared them to observed dates of first calling across 111 route-years spanning 90 routes and 19 survey years. TS3 significantly predicted observed onset with a slope indistinguishable from 1:1, but observations occurred roughly 45–50 days later than predicted. Geographic position alone explained more variance than TS3 (R² = 0.368 versus 0.177), and although adding TS3 to a model containing latitude and longitude improved fit, its coefficient was negative, contrary to the direction implied by the thermal-sum mechanism. Holding TS3 constant, onset occurred approximately 9.6 days later per degree of latitude north, indicating that factors covarying with latitude but not with accumulated warmth contribute to onset timing. Only 4% of peeper route-years met the criteria for observing an onset transition, and of those, 53% recorded a full chorus at first detection. These results quantify the gap between NAAMP's design, which targets peak calling rather than first calls, and what can be resolved about calling onset.
